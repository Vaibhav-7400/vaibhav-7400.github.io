Inventory Policy & Cost Optimization

An EOQ and safety-stock model that translates retail transactions into SKU-level ordering policies and service-level tradeoffs.

Working dashboard
PythonEOQABC analysisStreamlit

The engineering problem

Inventory decisions must balance availability, ordering cost, and capital tied up in stock. This project uses public Online Retail transactions to develop reorder policies and identify high-value SKUs.

The approach

  1. Clean transaction history

    Remove cancellations, duplicates, non-positive quantities and prices, and non-product codes before modeling demand.

  2. Calculate ordering policies

    Estimate SKU-level demand, economic order quantity, safety stock, and reorder points under explicit cost and lead-time assumptions.

  3. Segment the portfolio

    Apply ABC classification to prioritize inventory attention using annual revenue contribution.

  4. Compare cost and service

    Compare the policy with a monthly-ordering baseline and examine how service level and lead time change inventory requirements.

What the project delivers

The repository documents policies for 3,659 SKUs from 391,150 modeled transactions, with an interactive dashboard and cost-sensitivity views. Savings are model projections against a defined baseline, not realized business savings.

Data & interpretation

Ordering cost, holding cost, lead time, and service targets are assumptions because the transaction dataset does not contain those fields. The proposed policy needs operational validation.

The next engineering step

Validate lead-time and demand assumptions, add supplier constraints, and backtest stockout performance.
